It is the page someone lands on after typing a name into a search box, so it answers the question they arrived with — what is this, and is it for me — before offering anything to read. The list of everything lives one click away, where somebody who already knows the site goes looking for it. It carries no list of posts. A curated three lived here for a while and had the same problem in miniature that the full list had: a visitor who has not decided whether to read anything yet is being asked which thing to read. The two cards send them to the archive or to the author instead, and both of those pages are built to be arrived at cold. {%- endcomment -%}

Code is now the cheap part. What is not cheap is being able to say, at the end of a release, which behaviours were asked for, which an agent decided along the way, and which nobody decided at all.

That is the subject here: the specification, the decision record and the tests that make an AI-assisted delivery explainable. In a migration, where the specification has to be recovered from a system that already runs. In a greenfield build, where it is the only source there is. And in the case most teams are actually in — adopting agents a piece at a time, without a rewrite, while continuing to ship.

Everything here comes from projects that ran, and the clients stay unnamed. Where an article claims something works, it carries the figures or the artefact that came out of it. What is not here: model comparisons and tool benchmarks, which date in weeks, and prompt technique — a specification outlives the session, gets reviewed, and is versioned with the code.
